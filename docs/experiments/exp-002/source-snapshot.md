## PASS 1 — structure and rules

Key takeaways: cold email = targeted message to someone unfamiliar with sender,
to initiate a business conversation, not close a sale. Average reply rate ~3.4%;
strong 5-8%; exceptional >10%. Optimal length 50-100 words for most initial messages.

Six-step framework:
1. From line — format matches tone: "First and last name" for standard B2B;
   "First name at Company" conversational.
2. Subject line — specific and relevant ("Your new SDR team", "Automating
   follow-ups at {{COMPANY}}?"); avoid vague phrases like "Quick question."
3. Introduction — begin with the recipient's situation, not yours; reference an
   observable signal (hiring activity, product launches) connecting to the offer.
4. Benefit statement — one advantage tied to the identified problem; outcomes
   over features.
5. Call to action — one clear, proportionate request ("Does this match what
   you're experiencing?" over 45-minute demo asks to strangers).
6. Signature — full name, title, company, one relevant link, optional phone;
   minimal professional design.

2026 changes: generic openers ("I hope this email finds you well," "I came
across your company") no longer resonate; template-based personalization is
recognized instantly; advantage belongs to genuine research and specific
knowledge. AI's role: research acceleration and signal organization, not
generating complete messages; verify facts, edit suggestions.

Follow-up strategy: 2-3 follow-ups, each a distinct reason to respond; stop
on reply; same thread unless context requires new subject.

Measure replies and outcomes, not open rates alone.

## PASS 2 — verbatim details

Templates (6, verbatim): SaaS sales ("Hi {{FIRST_NAME}}, I noticed {{COMPANY}}
is hiring several SDRs while expanding into {{MARKET}}. Teams at that stage
often need to increase outbound activity before every new rep is fully
productive. Woodpecker automates personalized follow-ups, so existing reps
spend less time scheduling sends and more time handling replies. Does that
match what your team is experiencing?"); Agency (specific observation →
company-type problem → similar client + result + one-sentence method → "Would
it be useful if I sent over the main changes we made?"); Recruiting (specific
project/achievement + relevant detail → role + main responsibility →
connection → "Would you be open to a short conversation about the role?");
Referral (mutual contact + specific initiative → similar company + related
problem + specific result → "Would a short conversation make sense, or is
someone else on the team closer to this area?"); Partnership (shared audience,
different parts of related process → specific partnership idea → "Would it be
worth comparing where the two offers overlap?"); Follow-up ("One detail that
may be relevant to the message below: {{NEW INSIGHT/RESULT/RESOURCE}}. It
connects directly to the {{PROBLEM}} I mentioned... Is this still a priority,
or should I close the loop for now?").

Subject lines: "Idea for scaling {{COMPANY}} outbound"; "Thought on
{{COMPANY}}'s {{CHANNEL}} results"; "{{ROLE}} opportunity at {{COMPANY}}";
"{{MUTUAL_CONTACT}} suggested I reach out"; "{{COMPANY}} and {{YOUR_COMPANY}}";
structures: "{{FIRST_NAME}}, idea for improving {{SPECIFIC_PROCESS}}";
"Question about {{COMPANY}}'s {{SPECIFIC_INITIATIVE}}"; "Your post on
{{SPECIFIC_TOPIC}}"; "Reducing {{SPECIFIC_PROBLEM}} at {{COMPANY}}".

Banned openers: "I hope this email finds you well," "I came across your
company," "I wanted to introduce myself," "We work with companies just like
yours," "Are you looking to increase revenue?"

Banned subject behaviors: pretending prior conversation; false urgency;
unrelated clickbait; newsletter-style headlines; overpromising vs body;
vague "Quick question."

Banned signature elements: several logos/large images; multiple social icons;
several promo links; long disclaimers; inspirational quotes; awards/banners;
heavy HTML.

Deliverability: spam causes = missing/incorrect SPF/DKIM/DMARC; invalid/
unverified addresses; sudden volume increases; poor sender/domain reputation;
repetitive content to large lists; excessive images/links/HTML; low
engagement; missing opt-out. Conservative daily limits, gradual ramp, natural
schedules.

Personalization: research sources = company website, job listings, LinkedIn
posts, interviews, product announcements, press releases, case studies,
public reviews, podcasts. Signals = launches, job ads, market expansion,
leadership changes, published content, interviews/conference appearances,
externally-visible business problem. "One relevant detail is enough."
"A template becomes useful only after it stops reading like a template."
Name+company+industry mail-merge is NOT deep personalization.

Length bands: <50 words easy to scan, may lack context; 50-100 enough context
without excessive reading; 100-125 when offer needs more explanation; 125-200
greater risk of losing attention; >200 usually too demanding for first touch.

Follow-ups: "two or three follow-ups are a practical starting point" (4 total
touches with initial); each adds new value; same thread; stop on reply.

Metrics: replies, positive replies, meetings, outcomes — not opens. Weak
performance diagnostic order: 1 deliverability, 2 list quality, 3 segment/
offer fit, 4 opening relevance, 5 value proposition, 6 CTA.

NOT PRESENT in article: subject character limits; days between follow-ups;
list size guidance.
